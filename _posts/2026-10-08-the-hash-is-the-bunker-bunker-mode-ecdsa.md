On Tuesday, October 7 at roughly 14:14 UTC, Justin Drake, Ethereum Foundation researcher and the industry's loudest q-day prophet, called on the blockchain world to begin planning "bunker mode": a calm, controlled migration of assets to fresh addresses whose public keys stay hidden behind hashes — because AI-driven mathematics, he argued, could break ECDSA, the signature scheme securing Bitcoin and Ethereum, before any quantum computer does, in a worst case "within months, not years."[^1][^2] The trigger was upstream: two days earlier OpenAI had dumped 722 manuscripts of machine-found mathematics into a public repository, integer multiplication faster than n·log n included, and Drake's post read the shockwave — "recent days have been humbling for human mathematical intuition. Long-held, unquestioned hypotheses have fallen."[^3] Vitalik Buterin answered within hours: confirm multisig transactions offchain so exposed signatures degrade gracefully to a 1-of-1 — "a much better place to be than 'anyone can take the money'" — while warning that botched migrations lose funds with certainty.[^4] Nothing has broken. The industry split between preparing and arguing about preparing.[^5] Yesterday this blog watched mathematics acquire a referee queue. Today, an entire asset class re-priced its assumptions in an afternoon. Four frames.

## Assumption Audit — the memo audits itself

Bunker mode is a rare crisis plan that names its own keystone: "my inclination is to go all-in on hash-based cryptography and avoid structured mathematical assumptions entirely."[^2] The register writes itself. The definitional move — reframing the threat from "ECDSA breaks by q-day" to "ECDSA breaks, full stop" — deletes the physical bottleneck from the threat model entirely. The causal keystone: that AI's demonstrated gains in number theory and algorithms *transfer* to the elliptic-curve discrete log problem. Load: the entire urgency of the memo. Confidence: low — no published attack, no known route; the inference is analogical, not evidential. Testability: cheap, and already running — discrete-log progress appearing in the Lean-verified subset of OpenAI's release would move it fast. The people assumption is sharper than it looks: that a "controlled" migration stays controlled once announced, when every exposed pubkey is publicly enumerable and a migration publishes a worklist of which vaults still sit behind the old lock. And a continuity assumption Drake needs but cannot have: that the math acceleration persists rather than spiking. His urgency collapses if last week was a spike.

## First Principles — what a chain actually stands on

Strip it down. On-chain ownership is a claim enforced by mathematics, and the mathematics comes in exactly two structural classes. Structured assumptions — discrete log, factoring — have algebraic shape: progress means finding the right model, and a found model replicates for free. Unstructured assumptions — hash functions — have no known shape; the best known attacks are generic search, so intelligence has nothing to grip. ECDSA's design forces every account to reveal its structured surface — the public key — at first signature; the hash address hides the key behind a one-way function instead. The inherited disaster plan assumed the constraint on breaking crypto was physical: qubits take decades. First principles say the constraint was never physics, it was insight — and OpenAI's own accounting pegged insight at roughly three hours of compute per result.[^3] Rebuilt conclusion: price the assumption, not the hardware, and prefer assumptions that are inert to intelligence. That is Drake's argument with the jargon removed, and it is why people who will never hold a crypto wallet should still read a post about Ethereum keys.

## Inversion — how to guarantee bunker mode kills the chain

Goal: survive a possible ECDSA break. Invert. Failure mode one: migrate everyone at once — the migration becomes the attack, botched transfers and scams liquidating funds before any mathematics does, which is precisely Buterin's certainty-weighted objection.[^4] Two: publish the safe-address list and hand attackers an inventory of who has not moved. Three: wait for industry consensus, the artisanal-referee bottleneck from yesterday's post wearing a wallet. Four: overcorrect, abandoning all structured assumptions and with them zero-knowledge proofs and signature aggregation — safety purchased with the chain's performance budget. Five, self-inflicted: attach a date. A months-scale deadline that passes uneventfully teaches the industry to ignore the next warning. The guards: cohort-staged migration with large holders first — Drake's own sequencing[^1] — offchain multisig for graceful degradation, migration tooling rehearsed in calm weather, and warnings framed as probability updates rather than countdowns. "Calmly" is doing load-bearing work in the original memo; it is what separates a drill from a stampede.

## Ladder of Abstraction — from one post to a design principle

Down the ladder: a researcher posts wallet hygiene advice on X on a Wednesday. Middle: crypto is not uniquely vulnerable — banks run ECDSA, TLS runs on elliptic curves — it is uniquely *exposed*, because chain assets are instantly, irrevocably, and pseudonymously stealable the moment the math falls. It is the canary with the shortest fuse; every other structured-assumption system inherits the same re-pricing with a lag. Up the ladder: when the generation of mathematical results accelerates, the half-life of a structured assumption shortens, and system design should classify assumptions by structure rather than by age — the hypotheses that fell this week had stood for decades, and seniority is not evidence of hardness. The principle: prefer assumptions that are inert to intelligence, and keep an exit rehearsed for the ones you cannot avoid. Check against the concrete: faster-than-n·log-n multiplication was itself a structure assumption about algorithmic lower bounds, fallen on Monday; Drake's post is this ladder walked in one afternoon. It holds.

## What the frames show together

Nothing broke this week, and that is the news. The referee queue and the bunker memo are one event seen from both ends: industrial conjecture forces every trust system to re-derive itself. The audit shows the memo is honest about its keystone; first principles show "avoid structure" is design discipline, not paranoia; inversion shows the migration is currently more dangerous than the break it fears; the ladder shows crypto is merely first in line. September's binding constraint in mathematics was refereeing; the standing constraint in cryptography was insight. Both moved in the same week. Everything priced to the old constraint is repricing in public — chains first, on a schedule measured now in referee queues and repo commits rather than qubit counts.
