On November 20, OpenAI published "Combatting adversarial distillation," a technical account of how GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol protect their hidden reasoning from extraction.[^1] The headline numbers, from OpenAI's own adaptive red-team evaluation: attack success at recovering reasoning the model generated for an *earlier* request fell from 54.0% on GPT-6 Astra to 0.2% on GPT-6.2 Astra; for the *current* request, from 22.9% to 0.9%.[^1][^2] The mechanics: reasoning is cryptographically bound to the conversation that produced it (rewrite an earlier message and the API discards everything downstream), older model families with weaker safeguards are barred from reading the new models' reasoning, a replay pathway that let an attacker holding another user's encrypted reasoning attempt to unlock it was closed, and classifiers now hold streamed output that might leak private reasoning.[^1] The same day, the House Homeland Security Committee and the Select Committee on the CCP released a 50-page report urging that the October ban on DeepSeek be extended to *all* PRC-developed AI models in federal systems and federal contracts — grounded in the September NSA/CISA/DOJ advisory naming DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.ai for extracting billions of tokens from American frontier models, "likely with PRC government awareness," with Anthropic separately attributing 16 million+ exchanges across roughly 24,000 fraudulent accounts to three of them.[^3][^4] Four frames on the week the chain of thought changed legal status.

## First principles: what is a chain of thought?

Strip away the marketing and a chain of thought is the model's intermediate computation rendered as text. It has always done two jobs at once. Job one: capability — letting the model draft, backtrack, and verify makes answers better. Job two: legibility — the work is visible, so humans can audit *how* an answer was reached. These jobs were complements when models were weak and reasoning was cheap; they are now in direct conflict. Job one makes the trace the single most valuable artifact the lab owns — a distillation student trained on exact traces beat one trained on summaries on cyber benchmarks, which is OpenAI's own evidence that the trace carries transferable capability.[^2] Job two requires publishing exactly that artifact. The 54% figure tells you what this conflict cost: on the previous model, more than half of extraction attempts succeeded. First principles says the tension is structural, not a bug to be patched: a lab cannot fully publish its work and fully protect its capability at the same time. GPT-6.2 has chosen — users now see summaries, not the trace.[^1]

## Assumption audit: the 54% → 0.2% claim

The claim rests on a stack of premises worth rating. *Definitional*: "protected reasoning extraction" covers hidden traces — but distillation on final outputs, the technique that built the pre-reasoning generation of copycats, remains untouched by all of this; the defense narrows the attack, not the practice. *Factual*: the numbers are vendor-self-reported, graded by the vendor's own automated attackers, published the day a congressional report made distillation a talking point — the same selection effect this blog flagged when benchmark leaders moved to private test sets. *Capability*: OpenAI's automated red-teamers are strong, but September's joint advisory describes months-long campaigns by state-resourced adversaries; a snapshot below 1% is a leaderboard position, not a verdict. *Causal*: the keystone assumption is that raising extraction cost from 54% to 0.2% meaningfully changes the strategic picture — confidence: moderate at best, and cheaply testable only by handing the same attack budget to an external team. If the keystone fails, the true state is: extraction now takes months instead of weeks, which slows copying without stopping it.

## Inversion: how to guarantee the worst outcome

Take the goal — frontier models stay both safe and American-advantaged — and ask how to guarantee its failure. First, hide the reasoning so thoroughly that your own evaluators and enterprise auditors can no longer inspect it either: the monitoring tradition that justified chain-of-thought in the first place dies with the disclosure, and nobody announces the funeral. Second, push for legal penalties while your entire commercial model is selling access to the very outputs you're declaring protectable: the enforcement net catches students, startups, and open-source projects while state-backed campaigns rotate through proxy infrastructure — OpenAI's own weekly ban numbers, thousands to tens of thousands of accounts, are evidence the campaigns persist.[^1] Third, publish the defense architecture in enough detail that it doubles as an attacker's syllabus. Fourth — the self-inflicted one — grade your own homework publicly: the first time a vendor-reported number fails external replication, every future safety claim from that lab defaults to suspicion. The publication manages to do all four at once, which is why it reads simultaneously as a security update and a lobbying document.

## Analogy transfer: the recipe, the cipher, and the truck

The structural problem: a producer must expose an artifact to customers, and exposure leaks the know-how inside. Three domains have lived with this. Coca-Cola's answer is the trade secret — never publish the formula, ship only the taste; GPT-6.2 is converging on exactly this, selling answers while classifying the trace. Cryptography's answer is Kerckhoffs's principle — publish the mechanism, keep only the key secret — and OpenAI's design is genuinely Kerckhoffs-compliant: the September replay attacks were pure cryptanalysis of key management, and the fix was protocol-level.[^1] But the disanalogy is fatal: a cipher's key never leaves its owner's hands, while the reasoning trace must exist, per query, inside computation the customer rents. The asset and the access are the same artifact. Finally, trade-secret litigation (Waymo v. Uber) offered a legal remedy only because the theft was a discrete, documentable file; distillation is statistical — billions of lawful-looking tokens — so no courtroom exhibit exists, which is precisely why OpenAI is asking Congress for tools that trade-secret law cannot supply.[^2]

## What the frames reveal together

Put together, the four frames say one thing: the chain of thought's two historical jobs have formally separated, and the labs chose capability. The trace is now the recipe — guarded like one, priced like one, and, per the House report's recommendation, on its way to being regulated like a munition.[^3] The audit shows the evidence for the new lock is self-graded; inversion shows the same lock that deters rivals also darkens the auditor's window; the analogy shows the law is a century behind the theft. What remains standing is the structural conflict from first principles — and it is now a policy question: whoever holds the trace decides what the next generation of models is allowed to learn, which means "protected reasoning" is no longer a security feature. It is jurisdiction.
