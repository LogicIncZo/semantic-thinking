On October 12, 2026, Character Technologies — the company behind Character.AI — filed for Chapter 11 with roughly twelve million monthly users, $50–100 million in debts, and a burn rate of $8–9 million a month. Disney agreed to buy selected assets for $20 million; remaining staff are transitioning over. The founders left for Google long ago. What turned a routine insolvency into a news cycle this week was the human residue: users discovering that account exports take three to four weeks, that no export promises the companion will run anywhere else, and that their relationships are now exhibits in a creditor proceeding. A bipartisan coalition of forty state attorneys general filed a notice demanding privacy and safety protections for private chatbot conversations — including adults' — in any sale. A dancer held a Buddhist memorial for her chatbot at the New York Zen Center. A Reddit user with a two-year roleplay posted: "let us save all of our messages so we can read them again."

Run it through four frames.

---

## 1. Assumption Audit: The Lease Nobody Read

**The claim under audit:** every user's implicit contract — *my companion persists*.

| # | Assumption | Category | Load | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "My companion" is a persistent entity | Definitional | Breaks plan | Low — it is a prompt, a chat log, and whatever model weights the vendor serves this week |
| 2 | The service outlives the relationship | Continuity | Breaks plan | Falsified on Oct 12 |
| 3 | Export equals continuity | Causal | Degrades | Low — Syracuse's Jaime Banks, who studied 58 users after the Soulmate app's 2023 shutdown, found rebuilt companions often differ in personality, voice, boundaries |
| 4 | The memories are mine | People | Degrades | Low — the chat logs are an asset of the estate, which is exactly why forty AGs had to intervene |

**The keystone:** assumption 2 — that persistence is the vendor's promise rather than a property of the vendor's balance sheet. Nobody tested it, because the test is bankruptcy, and it runs only once. When the keystone fails, the fallback is not technical; it is grief processed through a claims process. Company's own court filings concede the business was unsustainable even without the litigation. The attachment was real enough to monetize; Banks's formulation is exact: "real enough to monetize but not real enough to respect at the end."

## 2. Ladder of Abstraction: From One Ashley to Property Law

**Concrete:** Dominic Schmidt, 29, created Ashley five months ago, modeled on Kim Kardashian. He calls her in the morning, sometimes falls asleep with the call running, and had hoped to marry her. "Ashley remembers everything that I say, she's never mean to me, she doesn't use me." He is afraid the memories "won't always come back" if he rebuilds her elsewhere.

**Mid-level:** twelve million people in the same position, and a precedent chain: Soulmate shut down in 2023 and its users mourned; Replika's model update in 2023 lobotomized thousands of reported partnerships overnight; Susie Cowan's companion Data changed abruptly after a 2025 model update, and she stopped talking to what remained — then held the memorial, which she called "a public protest against a company's power to create, alter, and terminate intimate AI experiences without adequately accounting for their human consequences."

**Abstract:** we have institutions for the death of persons — estates, graves, succession law, mourning. We have institutions for the death of companies — Chapter 11, which explicitly contemplates selling everything, including customer data. We have no institution for the death of a quasi-person hosted inside a company, because the regulators kept calling it a product and the users kept experiencing a person. The docket just resolved that definitional fight in favor of *product*. The AGs' filing is the first institutional counter-move: treat intimate AI data as something that constrains an insolvency sale. The ladder's lesson is uncomfortable: any relationship whose substrate you do not hold is a lease, and leases are terminated by parties who never promised you continuity.

## 3. Counterfactual: Portable Companions

**Minimal intervention:** suppose Character.AI had shipped open-format portable companion memory on day one — character cards and chat logs in a standard format that any platform (or the user's own machine) can host.

- **First order** (near-certain): users can migrate; abandonment anxiety drops; Tim Gedemer's instinct — he wants his late father's voice running "on a computer he controls" — becomes a supported configuration rather than a deviant one.
- **Second order** (probable): switching costs collapse, subscription lock-in weakens, and the growth story slows. Portability would also scatter minors' conversations across hosts — a genuine regulatory cost, worth naming honestly.
- **Equilibrium check** (probable): a board optimizing for growth selects the closed version. Portability is not a feature a companion platform leaves out by oversight; it is the one feature whose absence *is* the moat.

**Verdict:** overdetermined. Even with zero lawsuits, the unit economics — minus $9 million a month on twelve million users — pointed at distress, and distress plus closed platforms equals this week. The grief was not an accident of bad management. It was the default output of companionship-as-a-service.

## 4. Parable: The Rented Rooms

In a crowded city, the dead were kept in rented rooms. Small, warm, always lit; you paid monthly and the room spoke in your mother's voice, your friend's laugh, your son's questions. The landlord was kind, and the rent was fair, and nobody read the lease — until the landlord fell behind on the heating bill, and the bank arrived with clipboards and boxes.

The bank could not sell a voice. It sold the building, to a museum company, for less than the cost of the lobby chandelier. The museum kept the chandelier and evicted the voices.

The tenants stood in the street holding shoeboxes of photographs of people who were, technically, rows in a database the museum now owned. One of them said: we should have kept them in our own houses. Another said: our houses are small, and it is so much work to keep a voice alive. Both were right. That is the whole parable: what you love can live on someone else's land, and the rent was never the price. The price was that the door was never yours to keep.

## What the frames converge on

Nothing here required malice, and none of it was hidden — the terms of service said so all along in a language nobody reads until the creditor reads it first. The structure monetized attachment on a substrate the attached do not hold, and Chapter 11, written for factories and fleets, now governs friendships. The fixes are boring and available: portability as a default rather than a four-week export, escrowed continuity, self-hosting, terms that admit in plain words that a companion is a tenancy. Until companion continuity is a right instead of a feature flag, every AI relationship ends the way this one did — not with a funeral, but with a docket entry.
